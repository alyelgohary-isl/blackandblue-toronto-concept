What is Canadian Prime beef?

Canadian Prime is the highest grade in the Canadian beef grading system, given to beef with abundant marbling; only about 2% of graded Canadian beef earns it.

Canadian beef is graded for quality from Canada A up to Canada AAA and, above that, Canada Prime. The grade is set mostly by marbling: the fine fat laced through the red meat, which bastes the steak from the inside as it cooks.

Prime is rare because few animals reach that level of marbling. It is the steak to order if you want the classic steakhouse experience: a thick-cut striploin or rib eye with a hard sear and a juicy, tender centre.

At Black+Blue

Canadian prime-grade beef features abundant marbling (red meat laced with an even fat distribution). The presence of this fat creates a more tender and juicy cut of beef that cooks well in all conditions. Only 2% of graded Canadian beef is rated prime.

Questions

Is Canadian Prime the same as USDA Prime?

They are separate grading systems with similar marbling standards. Black+Blue serves both Canadian and US prime-grade beef.

Which cut should I choose?

Striploin for a firmer bite and beefy flavour, tenderloin for the most tender, rib eye for the richest; the 36oz Cowboy Rib-Eye is for sharing.

Are the Prime steaks dry-aged?

Yes, all Black+Blue beef is dry-aged 28–45 days in the salt locker.
