Black+Blue/Japanese A5 Wagyu

What does A5 Wagyu mean?

A5 is the top grade in the Japanese beef grading system: “A” for the highest yield and “5” for the highest quality, which requires exceptional marbling; at Black+Blue, in Toronto and Vancouver, every Japanese Wagyu cut is A5 with a marbling score of 8–10.

Japanese beef is graded on two scales. The letter (A to C) measures how much usable meat the carcass yields; the number (1 to 5) measures quality: marbling, colour, firmness and the fat itself. A5 is the best of both.

Marbling is scored separately on the Beef Marbling Standard (BMS), from 1 to 12. A5 starts at 8, so “A5, BMS 8–10” describes beef with dense, fine marbling that cooks quickly and eats rich: a few ounces is a serving.

Japanese Wagyu cattle are known worldwide for producing meat with exceptional marbling, naturally enhanced flavour, tenderness and juiciness. All of our Japanese Wagyu cuts have a quality score of A5 and a marbling score between 8-10, including the ultra-premium Miyazaki Wagyu.

Questions

Is A5 Wagyu the same as Kobe beef?

No. A5 is a grade any Japanese Wagyu can earn; Kobe is a protected origin, and Kobe beef must also reach quality grade 4 or 5 with a marbling score of 6 or more.

How much A5 Wagyu should I order?

It is very rich. Many guests share three to six ounces as a course alongside a Canadian Prime steak.

Can I try A5 Wagyu without ordering a steak?

Yes: the Wagyu nigiri on the raw bar, the Black+Blue Surf & Turf roll, and the Wagyu Hot Stone at Happy Hour, cooked on a salt brick.
