Black+Blue/Japanese Kobe Beef

What is Kobe beef, and where can you eat it in Toronto?

Kobe beef is Wagyu from Tajima cattle raised in Hyogo Prefecture, Japan, sold only through certified members of the Kobe Beef Marketing & Distribution Promotion Association; Black+Blue in Toronto and Vancouver are certified Kobe-Beef restaurants.

Kobe is not a breed but a protected name. To carry it, the beef has to come from Tajima-strain Japanese Black cattle born, raised and processed in Hyogo Prefecture and meet strict marbling and quality thresholds. Restaurants that sell it are licensed and display a bronze bull and a numbered certificate.

What sets it apart is the marbling the Japanese call shimofuri: fine, even threads of fat through the muscle. That fat melts at a low temperature, which is why Kobe is served in small portions, cooked quickly, and priced by the ounce.

At Black+Blue

Black+Blue takes great pride in being one of the select few dining rooms in North America that offers certified Kobe beef ribeye and striploin cuts. The secret behind this prestige cut of meat is the umami flavour that’s embedded in the “shimofuri” marbling.

Questions

Is Kobe beef the same as Wagyu?

All Kobe beef is Wagyu, but very little Wagyu is Kobe. Kobe is the certified name for Tajima Wagyu from Hyogo Prefecture.

How is Kobe beef priced at Black+Blue?

By the ounce: rib eye 52, striploin 56 and tenderloin 69 per ounce, with tenderloin in limited availability.

Do I need to reserve Kobe beef in advance?

Kobe is on the dinner and lunch menus; for a large party or a specific cut, mention it when you book a table.
