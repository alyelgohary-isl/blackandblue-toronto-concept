Black+Blue/Dry-Aged Beef

What is dry-aged steak?

Dry-aged steak is beef rested uncovered in a cold, controlled locker for weeks, so moisture evaporates and natural enzymes tenderise the meat; at Black+Blue, in Toronto and Vancouver, every steak is dry-aged 28–45 days in a Himalayan salt-brick locker in the dining room.

Fresh beef is mostly water. Hung on open racks at just above freezing, with air moving around it, a whole cut slowly loses moisture and concentrates; the meat’s own enzymes break down connective tissue at the same time. The result is a firmer, beefier, nuttier steak that is noticeably more tender.

Ageing is measured in days. Around four weeks the texture changes most; past five or six weeks the flavour becomes deeper and more funky. The dried outer crust is trimmed away before the steaks are cut, which is part of why dry-aged beef costs more.

At Black+Blue

We proudly display our beef in the centre of the dining room. The pale pink and orange wall is made of Himalayan salt bricks. The salt purifies the air and naturally removes moisture. This dry-aging process infuses an unforgettable flavour as it tenderizes the meat. All our steaks are kept in the cooler for 28-45 days and ensures a flavour concentration of the highest quality.

Questions

Can I see the salt locker?

Yes. It is in the centre of the dining room, and guests can receive an interactive experience with a Black+Blue butcher to learn more about the restaurant’s steak program.

Does salt-ageing make the steak salty?

No. The salt bricks draw moisture from the air in the locker and keep it clean; the beef is not salted by them.

How long is Black+Blue beef aged?

28 to 45 days.
