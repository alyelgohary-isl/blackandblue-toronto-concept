# Creative direction

Every site from this skill runs on the same engine, and visitors never see the engine. What they see is the look, and the look is the decision you take for this restaurant before any CSS. Changing hex values and font names inside last time's shell is a reskin. Three Masrawy directions were built before one landed; the client's words for the two that failed were "not unique", "AB AB palette" and "bloated". Read `past-directions.md` for what each one was, so you don't rebuild them.

## 1. Start from the restaurant, not the template

Answer these in `work/direction.md`:

1. **What is true of this restaurant that a competitor can't claim?** The only room of its kind in the city, the dish people film, the owner's story, the counter you sit at, the patio, the hour it comes alive, the thing they do every morning at six. One sentence.
2. **Who is eating, and what else do they look at?** Families from the community choosing Friday dinner on a phone (often in their own script), food people who saw a TikTok, an office manager booking lunch for forty, regulars who want today's special, a couple booking a party. Their world sets the register.
3. **An analogue from outside websites** that carries the feeling. Borrow its grammar (grid, type, rules, captions, margins, colour), not its content: a laid table (sofra, mezze, thali, omakase counter), a hand-painted shop sign, a paper placemat, a market stall at night, a recipe card, a wine list, a bakery's paper bag, a tasting-menu card, a 1970s cookbook, a bazaar lane, a steam-fogged window at 7am, a chalkboard, a street cart, a ticket stub, a butcher's paper wrap, a matchbook, a jukebox menu card, a hotel key folder, a noodle shop's vending-machine ticket. Pick one that fits *this* client; don't reuse one from `past-directions.md`.
4. **The idea, in one line.** "A bakery at the hour the ovens open." "The counter, seat by seat." "Friday night, table for twelve." "Order at the window." If the line could be pasted onto another restaurant's site unchanged, it isn't specific enough.
5. **Their brand and their words.** Logo, colours, typefaces people recognise, and the copy on their current site. The direction works inside the brand; the free axes are layout, scale, rhythm, framing and how the brand is used. Their current copy goes into the matching sections verbatim.

## 2. Decide each axis

For each row, write your choice and one clause on why it serves the idea. The middle column is what the template and the Masrawy builds did; it is listed so you notice when you drift into it.

| Axis | What has been done (don't repeat by accident) | Other directions |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | near-black green (template); plaster/umber bands (Masrawy v1); one night ground (v2); cream with the client's gold (v3) | paper white with one strong colour; butcher paper; chalkboard black with chalk type; a flat brand colour (terracotta, ultramarine, racing green) as the ground; bone with black rules; two grounds that mean something (day/night, front/back of house) |
| Type | display serif + humanist sans (template, Masrawy: Fraunces + Albert Sans) | one grotesk at extreme sizes; a condensed sans like a menu board; a slab; a single text serif throughout; a monospace for the menu beside a serif; hand-lettered display from the client's sign; the client's own menu typeface |
| Script | Arabic mark beside every dish name (Masrawy) | the script as the heading and English as the caption; script only in the wordmark; bilingual columns; no second script when the client doesn't use one |
| Scale and rhythm | big headings, generous spacing | oversized type cropping at the edges; dense catalogue rhythm (a menu is a catalogue); small type with huge margins; one giant number or word per screen |
| Grid | centred 1360 wrap, ruled rows (Masrawy) | asymmetric editorial columns; strict Swiss grid with visible rules; image-led full-bleed; an index or table; a narrow single column like a letter; a two-column menu card |
| Hero | full-bleed photo, copy panel left, three buttons (v3) | the menu itself as the hero; a 50/50 split; a typographic hero with the photo below; a centred wordmark over a loop; a letterboxed film with the copy under it; the hours and address as the hero (a neighbourhood place) |
| Film framing | square film card right (template); full-bleed wide + floating squares (Masrawy) | letterbox 21:9 with captions beneath; a centred square with type around it; a portrait card like a phone video; a strip of three squares; the film inside a shape (a plate, an arch, a window) |
| Beat copy | Arabic mark, dish name, menu line, price (v3) | plate numbers ("Plate 3"); a time of day per beat ("6:10 am"); one word; a running ticket; a caption bar like a subtitle; the menu section heading as the beat |
| Home sections | film → doors → marquee → signature list → room scrub → brunch band → proof → visit (v2); film → gallery → story → follow → reviews → hours (v3) | menu-first (the full menu on the home); a single featured dish and the hours; a day in the life (morning, lunch, night); locations as a map; today's specials from a data file; a letter from the owner; a reviews wall; nothing after the film but hours and a map |
| Components | ruled rows, pill buttons, chips, cards with hairlines | tabular lists; square corners; text links with arrows; image-only tiles; numbered items; tabs; a ticket-stub form; a chalkboard list |
| Image treatment | full colour, crisp, 4px corners or none | square and matted like prints; duotone in the brand colour; black and white with one colour; grain; tight crops on texture; circles for plates |
| Motion signature | pours and hands from their photos; cut rules; scrub heroes | text revealed through a mask; a horizontal pinned strip of plates; a clock dial tracking the film; a line drawing that fills; a counter of covers; a ticker of today's specials. One per site, used sparingly |

Fit to the business type: dine-in rooms lead with the room and the signature dish; take-out and fast casual surface the order button and the menu within one scroll; caterers lead with menus per person and the quote form; cafés lead with the morning; trucks lead with where they are today. Phone first: the mobile action bar is fixed on every site, with the actions this restaurant needs (Menu, Order, Call, Directions, Book).

## 3. Six directions that show the range

These are not a menu. They exist so "different" has a concrete meaning. If your direction sits closer to one of these than to the restaurant, go back to section 1.

- **A ramen counter, twelve seats.** Idea: "The counter, seat by seat." Analogue: the vending-machine ticket. Ground: warm white with a red stamp colour. Type: a condensed grotesk at poster sizes, numbers large. Hero: the twelve seats as a row of squares, each a clip (broth poured, noodles lifted, egg halved). Film: letterboxed over the counter, captions as ticket lines ("No. 4 · Tonkotsu · $18"). Home: counter → the four bowls → today's queue (hours, "last ticket 9:40") → map. Motion: a ticket prints as you scroll.
- **A French bakery, open at six.** Idea: "The hour the ovens open." Analogue: the paper bag. Ground: bag brown and flour white. Type: a single text serif, small, with huge margins. Hero: a square clip of the oven door with the time of day as the only copy. Film: 5:40, 6:10, 7:00, 8:30 as beats (dough, bake, the first customer, the empty tray). Home: film → the morning's list (what sells out when) → pre-order → the space. Motion: a clock dial.
- **A steakhouse.** Idea: "Dry-aged, counted in days." Analogue: a butcher's paper wrap and a wine list. Ground: deep oxblood with cream type. Type: a wide-caps display with a small text face. Hero: the wordmark over a loop of the fire, the reservation form in the hero. Film: the cut on the board, the fire, the plate, the room. Home: reserve → cuts as a table with days aged and price → wine → the room. Motion: a counter of days.
- **A taqueria, order at the window.** Idea: "Order at the window." Analogue: a hand-painted sign. Ground: a flat sign colour (sky blue or lime) with black. Type: hand-lettered display from their sign plus a mono for prices. Hero: the window with the menu board as the hero, order button first. Film: three squares in a strip (the trompo, the press, the salsa). Home: menu board → order → where we are today → gallery. Motion: a marquee of specials like the sign.
- **A tasting menu, twenty covers.** Idea: "Nine courses, one evening." Analogue: the tasting-menu card. Ground: bone with black rules. Type: one serif, numerals in old style. Hero: typographic, the nine courses listed; the photo comes after. Film: course by course, each a square, captions as course numbers. Home: the card → the chef's note (their words) → reserve → press. Motion: text revealed through a mask, nothing else.
- **A Caribbean takeout.** Idea: "Pepper, lime, line out the door." Analogue: a chalkboard. Ground: chalkboard black with chalk white and one hot colour. Type: a slab for headings, a rounded sans for the menu. Hero: today's specials from a data file, phone number big. Film: the pot, the plate box closing, the hands. Home: specials → the full menu → order → hours and parking. Motion: a ticker of what's sold out.

## 4. The sameness check

Compare your axis choices with every row in `past-directions.md` (this skill's and real-estate-website's) and with the six directions above. The new site must differ from each past row on at least 6 of the 12 axes, at least three of them among ground, type, hero, film framing and home sections. Write the comparison into `work/direction.md` as a short table. If it fails, change the direction, not the check.

"Like Masrawy", "like the one we did" and "same kind of scroll animations" mean the motion and the quality bar: keep the film mechanics, give the site its own look. Only reuse a past look when the user says so explicitly ("same design", "same look").

## 5. What read as template, in the client's own words

- Alternating light and dark bands with the same section order as the last build: "not unique", "AB AB palette".
- Seven sections after the film, every component the template offers: "bloated".
- A dark ground chosen because the last build was dark; ivory copy over a bright room photo: "difficult to read".
- Invented headlines in place of the client's own lines: "I don't like the new copy".
- Hard cuts between a full-bleed room and a floating square with no structure: "abrupt".
- A translucent cream gradient to make copy readable over the photo: "looks blurry".
- A gradient band under the header: "that line at the top is awkward".
What landed: the photo crisp edge to edge, copy on a solid panel in their words with their three buttons, each beat about the dish on screen with its menu line, a rule between clips, and a short page.

## 6. Show it at the gate

In the one gate message: the chosen direction as a short paragraph (idea, analogue, ground and palette, type, hero, framing, home sections), plus two alternates in one line each, and a 1440×900 screenshot of a static hero mock (one HTML file in `work/`, the client's photo, no generation). The mock is not optional: it is the only thing that stops the direction drifting back to the template once the CSS starts.

## Doing it in the template

- **Styles.** `:root` in `src/styles/style.css` is the minimum change, not the limit. Rewrite component rules, delete what you don't use, add your own. Rename the token names if they'd mislead (Masrawy v3 uses `--cream`, `--paper`, `--ink`, `--gold`); `ScrollFilm.astro` reads `--cream`, `--pine`, `--moss`, `--ink-soft`, `--hairline`, `--font-display` and `--gutter`, so alias those.
- **Markup.** `src/pages/index.astro` is a placeholder. Replace the sections to match the direction; add components in `src/components/`. Header, Footer and ActionBar are yours to restructure.
- **Film framing.** `bleedRect()` in `src/components/motion/scroll-film.js` places wide frames (cover, centred a little right) and square frames (right of the copy column, clear of the header); `frameRect()` is the card layout. Change them per site: letterbox, centred square, a strip, a shape mask. `--film-text-col` and `.band-section` in `ScrollFilm.astro` place the copy; override from `style.css`.
- **What stays.** The timeline and `data-seg` contract (including `cut` segments), `.band-section` and `.page-flow`, the loader and release behaviour, the reduced-motion poster, `url()`, the form handler and `data-form-type`. Change how they look, not how they work.
